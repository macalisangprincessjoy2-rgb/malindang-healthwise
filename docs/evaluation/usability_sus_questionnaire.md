# Usability Evaluation — System Usability Scale (SOP 5.1)

The System Usability Scale (SUS; Brooke, 1996) is a widely cited, validated
10-item instrument that produces a single comparable usability score per
respondent. It's the standard choice for a thesis usability chapter because
the scoring method and benchmark numbers are published and citable, rather
than something you have to justify from scratch.

## Administering it

- Give this **after** the respondent has completed at least one real
  assessment in the system (register → log symptoms → view the result and
  referral), not before.
- Respondents answer each item on a 1–5 scale (Strongly Disagree → Strongly
  Agree). Keep the odd/even statement pattern below exactly as written — SUS
  scoring depends on alternating positive/negative phrasing.
- Target respondents matching your actual users: barangay health workers and
  residents of Owayan, Hoyohoy, and the Don Victoriano barangays named in the
  dataset dictionary. Aim for at least 10–15 respondents; SUS is designed to
  work with small samples, but more respondents narrow your confidence
  interval.
- A bilingual (Bisaya/English) version is included below since your primary
  users are Bisaya speakers — offer both, or the Bisaya version alone if
  that's what respondents are more comfortable with. Keep the item order and
  polarity identical between languages.

## Questionnaire

For each statement, circle one: **1 = Strongly Disagree · 2 = Disagree · 3 = Neutral · 4 = Agree · 5 = Strongly Agree**

| # | English | Bisaya |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | I think I would like to use this system frequently. | Gusto ko nga magamit kini nga sistema kanunay. |
| 2 | I found the system unnecessarily complex. | Nasabtan nako nga lisod ug sobra ka komplikado ang sistema. |
| 3 | I thought the system was easy to use. | Sayon ra gamiton ang sistema, sa akong pagtuo. |
| 4 | I think I would need the support of a technical person to be able to use this system. | Kinahanglan nako og tabang sa usa ka technical nga tawo aron magamit kini nga sistema. |
| 5 | I found the various functions in this system were well integrated. | Ang lain-laing gamit sa sistema maayo nga nagkasumpay. |
| 6 | I thought there was too much inconsistency in this system. | Daghan kog nakit-an nga dili managsama o nagkasumpaki nga bahin sa sistema. |
| 7 | I would imagine that most people would learn to use this system very quickly. | Sa akong hunahuna, dali ra makat-on sa paggamit niini ang kadaghanan sa mga tawo. |
| 8 | I found the system very cumbersome/awkward to use. | Nabati nako nga lisod ug medyo hasol gamiton ang sistema. |
| 9 | I felt very confident using the system. | Kompiyansa ko nga makagamit sa sistema. |
| 10 | I needed to learn a lot of things before I could get going with this system. | Kinahanglan pa ko magkat-on ug daghang butang una ako makasugod paggamit sa sistema. |

## Scoring

1. For **odd-numbered items** (1, 3, 5, 7, 9): score = (respondent's rating − 1).
2. For **even-numbered items** (2, 4, 6, 8, 10): score = (5 − respondent's rating).
3. Sum all 10 adjusted scores (range 0–40), multiply by 2.5. That gives a
   0–100 SUS score for that respondent.
4. Average across all respondents for your reported usability score.

## Reporting benchmark

Report your mean score against the published benchmark (Bangor, Kortum &
Miller, 2008; Sauro, 2011): a mean SUS score around **68** is average, above
68 is above-average usability, and above ~80 is considered excellent. State
your sample size and mean alongside a short interpretation — don't just
report the number.
